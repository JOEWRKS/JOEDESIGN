# Produce the visual experience

Use for material visual direction and for a rejected proof whose central experience
needs rebuilding. Reuse the existing intent/lock/contract and actual assets; do not
add a second design system, compulsory ceremony or a new approval questionnaire.
The purpose is to improve what gets made, not the amount of supporting paperwork.

## Start with the visual bottleneck

Name what will carry this particular experience: for example an expressive specimen,
a dense but legible comparison, a photographic composition, or a continuous spatial
transition. Choose from the actual brief. Do not select a generic hero layout first
and force the content and reference into it.

Inspect the current output and reference at corresponding viewport/state. Identify
the largest visual gap. If the gap is focal imagery, test asset production; if spatial
composition, test the composition; if temporal continuity, test the sequence. Writing
navigation, footers, extra routes or an elaborate test suite does not reduce this risk.
Keep functional work proportional until this difficult part exists at useful fidelity.

## Observe enough to make a concrete translation

Open the user-pinned primary source. Use OMD to answer a specific remaining decision;
do not collect loosely related beautiful sites or let a secondary reference replace
the primary's assigned role. Stop research when the production decision is supported.

For an image/motion-led experience, pair actual source frames with the proposed scene:

- Composition: focal bounds and crop, text bounds/line breaks, negative space,
  foreground/background layering, where attention starts and where it goes next.
- Image treatment: silhouette, material/light, edge definition, texture density,
  depth cues and how the image relates to text. Color resemblance alone is insufficient.
- Motion: the trigger, reading holds and transition beats; which object persists;
  position/scale/crop/opacity through those beats; how copy enters/exits; interruption,
  reversal and reduced-motion alternatives. Inspect the sequence, not just endpoints.

Use normalized bounds or sketches where useful. Label measurements, estimates and
proposals honestly. Do not invent source easing/duration from a still. A video segment
or actual observed browser sequence can support motion; screenshots support only frames.
Keep original pointers beside the translation so a reviewer can check the interpretation.

Then map the source relationship to actual product meaning. What must the visitor
understand before/after the transition? What entity persists? What content is deferred
and where does it remain accessible? Preserve product facts/actions. A visual metaphor
must not silently relabel a biological entity as a research process step.

## Choose a production method by the required result

For the focal asset, compare the needed visual treatment with available real assets
and capabilities. Use suitable photography, illustration, generated imagery, SVG,
Canvas, 3D, shader or video work when appropriate; no medium is mandatory or inherently
low quality. Check asset rights and actual availability. For generated/edited imagery,
use the available image workflow rather than pretending code primitives are equivalent.

Prototype the uncertain material or motion treatment at its intended display size.
Judge its silhouette, depth, lighting, detail density, text relationship and behavior
against the chosen reference qualities. If the technique cannot produce those qualities,
change the technique or simplify the proposed direction explicitly. Do not silently
lower the visual target to whatever the first implementation makes convenient.

Mark temporary focal assets PROVISIONAL even if technically original and fully rendered.
Technical originality, a valid license and successful canvas drawing are not evidence
of visual adequacy. Conversely, do not reject a good simple vector solely for simplicity.

## Make the key scene and its transition before broad expansion

Use real headline/body lengths and intended fonts/assets. Compose the whole viewport,
including the next reading/interaction destination. Determine the focal dominance,
crop, type hierarchy and spacing together. A large heading next to an object is a
possible composition, not a default solution for every editorial or scientific site.

When the art direction is unresolved, compare a small set of materially different
visual treatments for the specific bottleneck. Avoid palette-only alternatives and
fixed candidate quotas. If an exact target is already approved, implement that target
instead of generating new directions.

In the target runtime, connect the key scene to the preceding/following beat. Use the
intended motion vocabulary and representative visual assets, not a placeholder zoom
whose endpoint alone works. Check the whole sequence in both directions and under
interruption. A reduced-motion static alternative is valuable but does not validate
the expressive sequence. Adapt the narrow layout deliberately after establishing
the core composition; do not defer all mobile evidence until full-page integration.

## Compare the actual experience, then change the right thing

Inspect source and candidate side by side at corresponding states, and watch their
sequences with the same task in mind. No pixel-copy requirement: compare the selected
relationships and the intended level of finish, while respecting different content.

Separate these judgments:

1. Conformance: did the implementation preserve the selected source relationships?
2. Whole-screen quality: do composition, focal asset, typography and motion work
   together with this actual content, beyond satisfying the written contract?
3. Function/accessibility: can the visitor operate and understand the experience?

State the dominant remaining gap with visible evidence. A compliant weak contract
can still produce a weak design. A functional transition can still have poor pacing.
Review whole compositions and moving sequences, not only circles, borders or isolated
type details. A static reviewer must explicitly leave motion unverified.

Match the repair to the diagnosis:

| Observed problem | Next experiment |
|---|---|
| Focal graphic lacks required material/depth/detail | Revise asset production or art direction at display scale |
| No clear focal hierarchy or weak composition | Recompose image/text/negative space before micro-spacing |
| Disconnected scenes or inert motion | Rebuild temporal beats, camera/object continuity and reading holds |
| Hierarchy succeeds but a local detail is wrong | Bounded type/crop/spacing correction and protected-region check |

Keep the rejected render and its source comparison. Do not treat two local fixes as
proof that the dominant gap disappeared. Explicit user rejection overrides internal
acceptance claims for that output. Record the affected decision and produce a materially
revised bounded candidate within authorization; do not merely rename or repackage it.

## Limits and handoff

The CLI can require coverage and block contradictory states. It cannot tell whether
an asset is compelling or authenticate an unobserved sequence. A filled contract,
test count, specialist name or review verdict is not proof of aesthetic success.
Absent calibrated/trusted review, present the proof as an experiment with observed
strengths and remaining gaps. Do not call it ready/ship/quality-passed just because
it runs. Do not fabricate source observations, user acceptance or host receipts.

Lead the handoff with the actual artifact and what visually changed. Put technical
checks after the visual outcome. Report the scope and the most material unresolved
gap plainly; do not bury it behind implementation detail or a long compliance list.
